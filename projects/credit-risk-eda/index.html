Overview

The Home Credit default dataset describes loan applicants and their repayment outcomes. The goal of this analysis is to understand the profile of borrowers who go on to default — and to do the groundwork properly, so that any model built on top of it is trustworthy rather than accidentally leaking the answer. Getting this stage right is what separates a model that looks good in testing from one that holds up on real applications.

How the EDA is structured

The analysis works through four passes over the data:

  • Data profiling — the shape, types, and integrity of each field.
  • Univariate analysis — the distribution of each feature on its own.
  • Correlation & relationships — how features move together and against default.
  • Missing-data assessment — where data is absent, and whether that absence is itself informative.

Guarding against leakage

A large part of the effort went into avoiding the two mistakes that quietly inflate model performance. The first is train/test contamination — letting information from the test set influence decisions made during training. The second is target leakage, which crept in through default-rate encoding: encoding a category by its observed default rate hands the model a peek at the outcome it is meant to predict. Both were identified and handled so the downstream model is evaluated honestly.

Cleaning & preparation

Alongside the analysis, the raw data was shaped into an analysis-ready state: datetime fields processed into usable form, duplicate records removed, and missingness assessed rather than blindly imputed.

Where this leads

This EDA is the first stage of a larger, staged build toward a full probability-of-default (PD), loss-given-default (LGD), and exposure-at-default (EAD) modelling project. The feature signal and leakage safeguards established here feed directly into that work.
